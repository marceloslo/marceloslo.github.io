I am an incoming Ph.D. student at Cornell's CS department, where I will work with [Prof. Cristian Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~cristian). There I will work on the development and auditing of AI mediators and assistants.

Previously I was an intern at MPI-SP under the supervision of [Prof. Mia Cha](https://www.mpi-sp.org/cha) and I completed my M.Sc. at UFMG under [Prof. Virgilio Almeida](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=sPKpIPwAAAAJ) and [Prof. Wagner Meira Jr.](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=oad6_oEAAAAJ) 
During my Master's I studied on analyzing collective behavior in social media platforms, analyzing interesting phenomena such as **politicization**, **misinformation** and **radicalization**.

**Research focus:** My focus with my research is understanding how "things" work. Here, a "thing" can be a machine learning model, social media platforms, or even human behavior. This interest molds my works, which generally fall into two (broad) areas:

- **Understanding AI models:** With the advances of large scale pretraining techniques, models are becoming increasingly large and complex. There is still a lack of understanding on how concepts are learned and internalized in these models and, in turn, a lack of understanding on how those concepts shape the model outputs, possibly leading to harm. At Cornell, I aim to further both of these goals through the study of conversational AI. At MPI-SP we have studied the model side of the equation ([ICML'26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07172)), while at UFMG we have studied the outputs ([AIES'24](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/view/31689)).

- **Understanding discourse in social media:** Social media has undeniably shaped how humans communicate, leading to a plethora of desirable -- and undesirable -- outcomes. Understanding how such outcomes takes shape is fundamental for **(a)** analyzing how people's behavior is changing in social media, **(b)** guiding efforts in platform moderation, **(c)** providing policymakers with actionable insights regarding social issues. At UFMG and MPI-SP, we have conducted important research regarding politicization ([ICWSM'24](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/31366)), misinformation ([FAccT'26](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3805689.3806492), [HyperText'22](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3511095.3531283)), climate communication ([AAAI'26](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/41180)), and radicalization ([ASONAM'25](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-13821-7_22)).
